I personally hope that this whole thing is a learning curve for companies/orgs/teams and also a differentiator. The ones using code generation carelessly might get a quick output boost, but eventually fall behind and (this is the hope part) go out of business because a buggy and messy codebase will eventually eat up the boost they got initially.

There's certainly some gains to be had, but far from what some claim and eventually, when companies will have to maintain their mess, the ones who actually care will come out ahead.
